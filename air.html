Air Pollution

Air pollution happens when harmful gases and small particles enter the atmosphere. Common sources include vehicles, industrial activities and burning waste.

Understanding air pollution

Causes

Most air pollution is created when fuel or waste is burned and the smoke and gases are released into the air.

  • Vehicle exhaust
  • Industrial emissions
  • Burning rubbish
  • Burning fossil fuels

Effects

Polluted air affects people as well as the natural environment, especially in busy towns and cities.

  • Breathing difficulties
  • Eye and throat irritation
  • Harm to human health
  • Environmental damage

Solutions

Air quality improves when less fuel is burned and when cleaner sources of energy are used.

  • Use shared transport
  • Reduce unnecessary vehicle journeys
  • Avoid burning rubbish
  • Support cleaner energy

Your Action

Students cannot control every journey, but they can help choose cleaner options when these are available.

  • Use school transport where possible
  • Carpool when practical
  • Walk or cycle when safe
  • Encourage responsible transport choices

School connection: how we travel

Every engine that burns petrol or diesel releases exhaust gases. When many students travel to the same place at the same time in separate cars, there are more vehicles on the road and more exhaust near the school gate.

A school bus carries many students in one vehicle, so shared transport can mean fewer journeys overall. Carpooling with a classmate who lives nearby works in the same way.

WHAT STUDENTS CAN DO

  • Use a refillable bottleRefill it at school instead of buying single-use plastic bottles.
  • Save waterTurn taps off fully and report any tap that is leaking.
  • Sort waste correctlyPut every wrapper, bottle and piece of paper in the correct bin.
