Water Pollution

Water pollution occurs when harmful substances such as chemicals, rubbish and wastewater enter water sources. This can affect aquatic life, ecosystems and the availability of clean water.

Understanding water pollution

Causes

Water becomes polluted when waste is allowed to reach rivers, lakes and oceans, often by travelling through drains.

  • Litter entering waterways
  • Chemical waste
  • Untreated wastewater
  • Waste entering drains

Effects

Polluted water is dangerous for the animals that live in it and for the people who depend on it.

  • Harm to aquatic animals
  • Unsafe water
  • Ecosystem damage
  • Reduced access to clean water

Solutions

Keeping waste out of water is far easier than cleaning water after it has been polluted.

  • Dispose of waste correctly
  • Keep rubbish away from drains
  • Support wastewater treatment
  • Reduce unnecessary water wastage

Your Action

Students use water at school every day, so careful habits here make a real difference.

  • Turn off taps after use
  • Report leaking taps
  • Keep school drains clear
  • Use water responsibly

School example: wasting water and polluting water are different problems

Water wastage means using more clean water than is needed, for example leaving a tap running at the water station or ignoring a leaking tap. The water is not dirty, but it is lost, and clean water takes energy and money to treat and supply.

Water pollution means harmful substances getting into water, for example litter washing into a drain and then into a river. The water is still there, but it is no longer safe for animals or people.

The school water station is a place to practise conservation: turn taps off fully and report leaks. Students help prevent pollution in a different way, by keeping litter away from drains so that rain cannot carry it into waterways.

WHAT STUDENTS CAN DO

  • Use a refillable bottleRefill it at school instead of buying single-use plastic bottles.
  • Save waterTurn taps off fully and report any tap that is leaking.
